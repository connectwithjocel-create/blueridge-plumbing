Overview

Water Heater Repair & Replacement in Austin, TX

Hot water is easy to take for granted until it runs out early, smells odd or stops altogether. Most tank water heaters last roughly 8–12 years, and Central Texas’ hard water can shorten that through sediment buildup. Knowing when to repair and when to replace saves money and prevents surprise failures.

Common Problems

  • Not enough hot water or water that cools quickly
  • Rumbling or popping noises from the tank
  • Rusty or discolored hot water
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater
  • Pilot or burner problems on gas units
  • Error codes on tankless systems

Potential Causes

  • Sediment and mineral buildup in the tank
  • Failing heating elements or thermostats
  • Worn anode rods leading to internal corrosion
  • Faulty temperature and pressure relief valves
  • Undersized units for the household’s demand
  • Scale buildup in tankless heat exchangers

How We Approach It

  1. 01

    Diagnose the unit and check safety components such as the relief valve and venting

  2. 02

    Repair elements, thermostats, valves or ignition parts where it makes sense

  3. 03

    Flush tanks and descale tankless units

  4. 04

    Size and plan replacements — tank or tankless — around how you use hot water

When to Call a Professional

  • Water is leaking from the tank itself
  • You smell gas near a gas water heater — leave the area and call your gas utility or 911
  • The relief valve discharges repeatedly
  • The unit is more than 10 years old and needs frequent repairs

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a water heater last?

Tank water heaters commonly last about 8–12 years; tankless units often last longer with regular descaling. Water quality, maintenance and installation all affect lifespan.

Should I flush my water heater?

Annual flushing helps remove sediment, especially with hard water. If a tank has never been flushed and is several years old, have it checked first — disturbed sediment can cause a drain valve to leak.

Is tankless better than a tank?

It depends on your home’s hot water use, fuel type, venting and budget. Tankless units save space and provide continuous hot water, while tank units cost less upfront and are simpler to replace.
