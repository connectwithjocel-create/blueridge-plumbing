Water Heater Maintenance: What Homeowners Should Know

Regular care extends water heater life. Learn the basics, the warning signs and how to decide between repair and replacement.

6 min readBlueRidge Plumbing Team

A water heater works hard every day and is easy to forget until something goes wrong. Basic maintenance and knowing the warning signs help you avoid cold showers and water damage.

Know your unit

Check the label on the side of the tank for its capacity, fuel type and manufacture date (often encoded in the serial number). Most tank water heaters last about 8–12 years.

Flushing and sediment

Hard water — common throughout Central Texas — leaves minerals that settle at the bottom of the tank. Sediment reduces efficiency and can cause rumbling or popping sounds. Annual flushing helps, but if a tank has gone years without it, have it checked first; disturbing heavy sediment can cause the drain valve to leak.

The anode rod

This metal rod inside the tank corrodes so the tank lining doesn’t. Inspecting and replacing it as needed can extend a tank’s life.

The temperature and pressure relief valve

This safety valve releases water if pressure or temperature gets too high. It should have a discharge pipe pointed toward the floor or a drain. If it drips or discharges often, have the system checked — don’t cap or plug it.

Temperature setting

Around 120°F is a common setting that balances comfort, efficiency and scald prevention.

Signs it may be time to replace

  • Water leaking from the tank itself
  • Rusty hot water
  • Frequent repairs on a unit older than 10 years
  • Not enough hot water for your household

Safety first with gas units

If you smell gas, leave the home immediately, avoid using switches or flames, and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Gas connections and venting should only be handled by qualified professionals.
