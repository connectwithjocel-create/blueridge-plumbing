How to Reduce the Risk of Clogged Drains

Most clogs are preventable. A few daily habits in the kitchen and bathroom keep drains moving and reduce emergency calls.

5 min readBlueRidge Plumbing Team

Clogged drains are one of the most common plumbing problems, and most of them are caused by what goes down the drain day after day. Small changes in routine can make a big difference.

In the kitchen

  • Keep grease out of the sink. Fats, oils and grease cool and harden inside pipes. Let them solidify in a container and throw them away.
  • Scrape plates into the trash. Rice, pasta, coffee grounds and fibrous vegetable scraps can build up even with a garbage disposal.
  • Run cold water with the disposal before, during and after use to carry waste through the line.
  • Use a sink strainer to catch food particles.

In the bathroom

  • Use hair catchers in showers and tubs and clean them regularly.
  • Only flush toilet paper. Wipes labeled “flushable,” paper towels and hygiene products don’t break down like toilet paper.
  • Clean pop-up stoppers in bathroom sinks every few months to remove hair and soap buildup.

Simple maintenance

Flushing drains with hot (not boiling) water can help move soap residue. Be cautious with chemical drain cleaners — they can damage some pipes, are hazardous to handle and often don’t fully clear a clog.

When a clog needs professional attention

  • Several fixtures back up at the same time
  • Water appears at a floor drain or cleanout
  • You notice sewage odors
  • The same drain clogs repeatedly

These signs often point to a blockage deeper in the line. Learn more about our drain cleaning service, or request an estimate.

This article is general information. For gas, electrical or other hazardous work, contact a qualified, licensed professional.
