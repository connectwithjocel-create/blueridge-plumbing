Overview

Plumbing Fixture Installation in Austin, TX

New fixtures change how a kitchen or bathroom looks and works — but only if they’re installed with the right valves, supply lines and drainage behind them. Careful installation protects cabinets and walls and makes future service easier.

Common Problems

  • Replacing worn or outdated faucets
  • Upgrading kitchen or bathroom sinks
  • Installing new shower valves and trim
  • Adding garbage disposals or water filtration
  • Fixtures that leak after a DIY installation
  • Planning plumbing for a remodel

Potential Causes

  • Worn internal parts in older fixtures
  • Mismatched valves and trim kits
  • Missing or seized shutoff valves
  • Incorrect drain heights or trap configurations
  • Fixtures not suited to the existing rough-in
  • Hard water shortening fixture life

How We Approach It

  1. 01

    Confirm compatibility between the new fixture and existing rough-in

  2. 02

    Replace shutoff valves and supply lines during installation

  3. 03

    Install, level, seal and test every connection

  4. 04

    Coordinate rough-in plumbing for remodels and new layouts

When to Call a Professional

  • A new fixture requires moving supply or drain lines
  • Shower valves need replacing behind the wall
  • Shutoff valves are corroded or won’t close
  • You want the installation done once and done right

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I reuse my existing shower valve?

Sometimes. Many trim kits only work with the same manufacturer’s valve. If you’re changing brands or styles, the valve behind the wall may need to be replaced.

Should shutoff valves be replaced with a new faucet?

It’s often a good idea. Older multi-turn valves can fail after years without use, and replacing them during the installation is quick.

Do you install fixtures I purchase myself?

Many homeowners buy their own fixtures. It helps to share the model numbers when you request an estimate so compatibility can be checked ahead of time.
